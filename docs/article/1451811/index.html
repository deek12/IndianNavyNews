Defence Minister Singh Emphasizes Holistic Approach to National Security

India’s Defence Minister Rajnath Singh has underscored the need for a comprehensive and inclusive approach to national security, highlighting the multifaceted threats the country now faces. Addressing a recent defense conclave, Singh stressed that India’s traditional focus on safeguarding geographical borders is no longer sufficient in the face of emerging security challenges. He identified several non-traditional threats, including cyber warfare, drone operations, economic vulnerabilities, and disruptions to energy supply chains.

Singh emphasized that modern warfare and security threats have transcended traditional battlegrounds. “To view national security solely in terms of safeguarding geographical boundaries amounts to a ‘Perimeter Fallacy’,” he declared, highlighting the need to address these new risks comprehensively.

The minister also underscored the importance of balancing diplomacy with military preparedness. He stated that neither military capability without diplomatic engagement nor diplomatic efforts without robust military strength could adequately protect national interests. Singh emphasized that India possesses both “Power of Argument” and “Argument of Power,” referring to the country’s ability to communicate through whichever language is understood by the other party.

Singh further stressed the importance of economic and energy security in bolstering national security. He explained that a robust economy provides the necessary resources for defense preparedness, while secure national environments facilitate development initiatives. Singh outlined the linkage between economic strength and military preparedness, asserting that a nation’s stability is a prerequisite for its development.

The minister also highlighted the critical factors of terrain, technology, and thinking in warfare. He proposed an investment in emerging technologies and the development of capabilities across drones, artificial intelligence, cyber security, electronic warfare, space, and quantum technology. Indigenous systems such as Rustom, Ghatak, and Nagastra UAVs have demonstrated India’s growing capabilities in unmanned warfare. Singh also mentioned the demonstration of drone warfare capabilities during Operation Sindoor.

Singh’s remarks included a separate discussion on the Indian Army’s recent mountaineering expedition. Defense Minister Rajnath Singh led a mountaineering team that successfully summited 20 previously unclimbed peaks above 6,000 meters in Ladakh in just 23 days, marking a historic achievement.

In another development, Singh announced a series of policy initiatives aimed at reducing barriers for small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) and start-ups, facilitating their integration into India’s defense manufacturing ecosystem. He stressed the importance of strengthening ties between the Defense Research and Development Organization (DRDO) and the private sector, particularly for MSMEs and start-ups.

These initiatives reflect India’s commitment to both advancing its own defense capabilities and fostering a robust and inclusive defense ecosystem. Singh’s emphasis on economic and energy security, alongside the need for a holistic approach to national security, underscores India’s strategic vision and preparedness for the evolving security landscape.
